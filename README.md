# Compilers, Codecs and Language Toolchains: What Agreement Between Builds Can Establish

Aaron Michael Weis · Oneiron Holdings LLC · [ORCID 0009-0002-1906-148X](https://orcid.org/0009-0002-1906-148X) · Research preprint v0.1.1 · 23 November 2026.

**[Read the PDF](compiler-toolchain-survey.pdf)** · [Read as text](PAPER.md) · [Explore the 73-entry catalogue](CATALOGUE.csv) · [Reproduce the experiment](experiment/README.md) · [Version and citation](https://github.com/OneiroNexus/compiler-toolchain-survey/releases/tag/v0.1.1)

Two compilers at two optimization settings agreed on every input, including on a deliberately wrong interpretation of a byte. Checking the answer against the field definition found 128 errors in each signed control. This preprint connects the distinction to a catalogue of 43 compiler and toolchain entries and 30 codec and format entries. In a proposed Omega Game Sphere trial, two players reach for the same virtual object. One player touches it first, yet the other player's message reaches the engine first. Aaron invites the Colorado Quantum Incubator timing team to help design a repeatable test of how closely two touches can be distinguished.

The full catalogue is inside the PDF as well as in the CSV. The numerical record is in [observations.csv](experiment/recorded/observations.csv) and [results.json](experiment/recorded/results.json).
